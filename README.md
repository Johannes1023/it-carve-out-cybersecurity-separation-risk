# ✂️ IT Carve-Out, Cybersecurity & Separation Risk

## 📌 Project Overview

This project focused on the IT separation of a business entity from its parent company.

The carve-out affected multiple business-critical systems supporting areas such as HR, sales and manufacturing. Applications, data, user access and external service provider relationships had to be separated while both organizations continued to operate independently.

I worked as the cybersecurity and technology risk expert across the carve-out workstreams and supported the project in identifying separation risks, clarifying ownership and defining requirements for a secure transition.

---

## 🎯 Business Challenge

The business entity relied on systems, data, identities, contracts and service providers that were historically shared with the parent organization.

The carve-out therefore required more than transferring individual applications. Technology dependencies had to be identified and separated without leaving inappropriate access, shared data or unresolved contractual responsibilities behind.

Key topics included:

- Separation of business applications and shared IT services
- Data ownership, migration and privacy
- Identity and access separation
- Third-party contracts and service provider dependencies
- Application and infrastructure ownership after separation
- Transition Service Agreements and exit planning
- Cybersecurity and technology risk during the transition

---

## 🧩 My Role

I worked across business, IT, cybersecurity, legal, privacy and external provider teams as the cybersecurity and technology risk expert for the carve-out.

## 🧩 Application & Service Separation

One of the first challenges was understanding which applications were dedicated to the carved-out entity and which were still shared with the parent company.

```text
Parent Company
      │
      ├── Shared HR Systems
      ├── Sales Platforms
      ├── Manufacturing Systems
      ├── Identity Services
      └── External Providers
              │
              ▼
         IT Carve-Out
              │
      ┌───────┴────────┐
      ▼                ▼
Parent Environment   New Entity
```

Together with application and business owners, we looked at how systems should be transferred, duplicated, replaced or temporarily provided through transition arrangements.

Important questions included:

- Who owns the application after separation?
- Which interfaces still connect both organizations?
- Which shared services need to be replaced?
- Which licenses or contracts need to be transferred?
- When can the dependency on the parent company be removed?

---

## 🔐 Identity & Access Separation

Access management was a major cybersecurity topic because employees, administrators and external providers historically used shared systems and identity services.

The carve-out required a clear separation of identities and permissions.

Key areas included:

- User account ownership
- Privileged and administrative access
- Access to shared applications
- External provider access
- Removal of legacy and cross-entity permissions

The objective was to ensure that users retained the access required for their new organization while preventing unauthorized access to systems or data belonging to the other entity.

---

## 🗄️ Data Separation & Privacy

Separating applications also meant separating the underlying data.

This was particularly relevant for HR, customer, sales and production information.

Together with data owners, privacy specialists and application teams, I reviewed topics such as:

- Data ownership
- Data migration and extraction
- Personal and confidential information
- Data retention requirements
- Access after separation
- Deletion or removal of data no longer required

The focus was on ensuring that each organization retained the data it legitimately required without leaving unnecessary copies or inappropriate access behind.

---

## 🤝 Service Providers & Contract Management

The carve-out also affected contracts with external technology and service providers.

Some contracts supported both organizations, while others needed to be transferred, renegotiated or replaced.

Key topics included:

- Contract ownership
- Provider access
- Service scope after separation
- License transfer and entitlement
- Contract novation or replacement
- Exit obligations

I worked with procurement, legal, IT and provider teams to understand where contractual dependencies could create technology or cybersecurity risk.

---

## 🔄 Transition Service Agreements & Exit Readiness

Not every shared service could be separated immediately.

Transition Service Agreements were therefore used to temporarily provide selected services while the new entity established its own technology capabilities.

```text
Shared Service
      │
      ▼
Transition Service Agreement
      │
      ▼
Temporary Dependency
      │
      ▼
Replacement / Migration
      │
      ▼
Exit
```

From a risk perspective, the important point was that temporary arrangements had clear ownership, access boundaries and exit criteria.

The goal was to avoid temporary dependencies becoming permanent because responsibilities or replacement solutions had not been defined.

---

## ⚠️ Cybersecurity & Separation Risk

The carve-out created a number of risks that were specific to organizational separation.

Examples included:

- Former employees retaining access to shared systems
- Privileged accounts remaining connected across entities
- Data being transferred to the wrong environment
- Shared applications continuing to expose information across organizational boundaries
- Third-party providers having unclear access or contractual responsibilities
- Critical dependencies remaining after the planned separation date

I worked with the relevant SMEs to identify these risks and prioritize remediation based on business impact and separation milestones.
